How the Two Games Differ in Play

In Mines, the danger is fixed the moment the round begins. The mines are placed, they never move, and every safe tile you reveal permanently shrinks the pool of remaining risk. Reading the round is about how deep to dig into a board whose contents were decided at the start.

In Floor Is Lava, the board itself is the opponent. Tiles sink every round — 7, 11, 23, or 48 of them depending on the difficulty — and no position is ever permanently safe, including the tile the egg is standing on. Surviving a round tells you nothing about the next one; and if you outlast the whole board, the game keeps going: the board resets, your multiplier carries over, and a new level begins, up to three per bet.

The practical difference: Mines rewards working a static board pick by pick. Floor Is Lava is a round-by-round survival check where the only real decisions are your difficulty, how many levels to chase, and your exit point.

Risk Control: A Dial vs. Presets

Mines gives you a 24-position dial. One mine on the board makes almost every pick safe and pays small; twenty-four mines makes the first pick a near-miracle and pays accordingly. You can tune the game to almost any risk appetite, and the multiplier table shifts with every setting.

Floor Is Lava compresses that into four presets. Easy (6 rounds per board), Medium (4), Hard (2), and Toxic (1) each have a fixed ladder, and clearing a board rolls you into the next level with the multiplier carried over — up to three levels per bet. The difficulty decides how brutal each survival check is; the levels decide how high the ladder can go. It is a simpler menu than Mines' dial: fewer knobs, faster decisions.

Payouts and RTP Compared

Mines has the edge on paper. Its multipliers are built on a 1% house edge (99% RTP), while Floor Is Lava's are built on 2% (98% RTP). Over the long run, that difference is real, if small.

The payout shapes differ more than the edges do. Floor Is Lava's ceilings are fixed per difficulty — 336.14× on Easy up to 115,296.02× on Toxic, reached by clearing all three levels — and its house edge is charged once, so advancing levels compounds your multiplier at fair odds. Mines' ceiling moves with your settings: the mine count reshapes the entire table, and a full clear on a high-mine board is its own long shot.
