How to Read College Football Odds

American odds usually appear as either positive or negative numbers.

Odds

Example Meaning

-150

Bet $150 to theoretically profit $100

+150

Bet $100 to theoretically profit $150

Stronger favorite

Negative odds generally indicate the favorite, while positive odds usually identify the underdog.

Odds also represent implied probability, although sportsbook margin means the combined implied probability across all possible outcomes will generally exceed 100%.

College Football Point Spread Betting

The college football spread gives one team a points handicap.

For example:

Team

Spread

Georgia

-10.5

Florida

+10.5

A Georgia -10.5 bet wins if Georgia wins by at least 11 points.

A Florida +10.5 bet wins if Florida either wins outright or loses by 10 points or fewer.

College football spreads can be considerably larger than NFL spreads because the difference in strength between NCAA teams can vary substantially.

The question is therefore not simply which team will win, but whether that team will cover the listed spread.

College Football Moneyline Betting

Moneyline betting removes the point handicap. You simply choose which team will win the game.

Rankings Do Not Tell the Whole Story

College football rankings provide useful context, but they should not be treated as betting lines.

A highly ranked team might win by 14 points and still fail to cover a -17.5 spread.

Consider Style Matchups

College football teams can use very different offensive and defensive systems.

A fast passing offense may face a defense designed to prevent explosive plays, while a run-heavy team could struggle against an opponent with a strong defensive front.

College football betting strategy should therefore consider how two teams match up rather than looking at win-loss records alone.

College Football Betting FAQ

What are the main college football betting markets?

Point spreads, moneylines and over/under totals are among the main NCAA football markets, alongside live betting, parlays and other available selections.

How does a college football spread work?

The favorite receives a negative points handicap and the underdog receives a positive handicap. The spread-adjusted final score determines which side covers.
