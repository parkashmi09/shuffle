Blitz Card Game Rules

The rules of Blitz fit in five lines:

  1. Before the round starts, choose your target: the number of unique cards you want drawn, anywhere from 5 to 36.

  2. Place your bet. Your potential payout is locked in the moment the round begins — each target has a fixed multiplier.

  3. Cards flip automatically, one at a time. Each card is drawn at random from a complete 52-card deck, independent of every previous draw.

  4. If the number of unique cards reaches your target with no repeats, you win your bet multiplied by the target's multiplier.

  5. If any card appears a second time before you reach the target, the round is over and the bet is lost.

Two details trip up new players. First, the deck does not shrink. Because every draw comes from the full 52 cards, a duplicate can land on the very second flip. Second, there is no decision to make once the cards start flipping — unlike Crash or Mines, you can't bail out mid-round. Your only lever is the target you set before betting.

An Example Round

Say you set a target of 7 unique cards, which pays 1.49x. The draw begins: 9♠, K♦, 2♥, 9♣... and the round continues, because 9♣ is a different card from 9♠ — only an exact repeat (the same rank and suit) counts as a duplicate. Three more flips land J♥, 4♦, A♠, and that's seven unique cards: the round pays 1.49x your stake.

Now imagine the same target, but the fourth flip is 9♠ again. The round ends immediately in a loss, even though you were over halfway there. That all-or-nothing structure is the core of Blitz's risk profile.

Is Blitz the Same as Dutch Blitz?

No. Dutch Blitz is a competitive tabletop game where players race to empty their "Blitz pile" by matching cards into shared stacks by color and sequence — it's about speed of hand and pattern recognition, and no money changes hands. Shuffle's Blitz is a single-player game of chance with real stakes, where the outcome is decided entirely by random draws. The same distinction applies to 3 Card Blitz (a poker-style table game) and Solitaire Blitz (a timed matching game). How Do You Know Blitz Is Fair?

Blitz is provably fair, which means you can independently verify that every draw was random rather than taking the casino's word for it. Each round's outcome is generated from three inputs: a server seed created by Shuffle, a client seed from your own browser, and a nonce (a counter that increments each round). Because your browser contributes one of the inputs, Shuffle cannot pre-determine or alter a result after your bet is placed.

After any round, you can check the result in Shuffle's verification tool. For a full walkthrough of how seed pairs and verification work, see our Provably Fair Guide.

Tips for Your First Blitz Session

  • Start at low targets. Targets of 5–8 cards win most rounds and let you learn the rhythm without volatility doing the teaching.

  • Treat high targets as lottery tickets. Anything past 20 cards wins rarely by design. Bet sizes there should be money you're entirely comfortable losing.

  • Know that no pattern helps. Every draw is independent and every round is generated fresh from the seed pair. Past rounds carry zero information about the next one, and no betting sequence changes the 2% house edge.

  • Decide your session budget before you start. Rounds take seconds, which is part of the fun — and exactly why a pre-set limit matters more here than in slower games.
