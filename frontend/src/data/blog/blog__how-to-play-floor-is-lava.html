How the Board Works

The board holds 49 tiles, and every round a fixed number of them sink permanently — 7 on Easy, 11 on Medium, 23 on Hard, and 48 at once on Toxic. The tiles that drop each round are chosen at random, and any tile can go, including the one the egg is standing on.

That last point shapes how the game plays. When you pick your next tile, you can move to a fresh tile, stay where you are, or return to any tile you have already landed on that has not yet sunk. None of those options is safer than the others: the danger comes from the drop, not the move. What changes is only where the egg is standing when the floor gives way.

How Levels Work

Levels are the game's progression system, and they sit on top of your difficulty choice. Survive every round on a board and the level is cleared: the board resets to a full 49 tiles, your multiplier carries straight over, and the ladder scales up. Each bet can climb through a maximum of three levels.

The size of the jump depends on your difficulty — the fewer tiles left standing when you clear a board, the bigger the scaling into the next one. That is why Easy's three-level ceiling is 336.14× while Toxic's is 115,296.02×: clearing Toxic means landing a 1-in-49 pick, and doing it three times in a row is rewarded accordingly.

Completing a level is also a natural checkpoint. Like any safe landing, it is a moment to bank the win before deciding whether the next board is worth the risk.

Cashing Out: When and How

After any safe landing, the cash-out option is live. Take it and the bet ends at your current multiplier; skip it and your stake rides into the next round. There is no penalty for cashing out early and no bonus for waiting — each round is an independent gamble at a fixed price.

The most useful habit is deciding your exit before you start. Pick a target multiplier or a number of rounds, and treat reaching it as the win. Because the board shrinks every round, the temptation to take "one more tile" grows exactly as the odds of surviving it worsen.

Floor Is Lava FAQ

Which difficulty is best for beginners?

Easy. Seven tiles drop per round instead of eleven or twenty-three, each board runs six rounds, and you get the most cash-out decisions per level, which is the best way to learn the game's pacing.

Do I have to move to a new tile every round?

No. You can stay on your current tile or return to any previously chosen tile that has not sunk. The odds of the next round are the same regardless of where the egg stands.

What happens if I never cash out?

The bet continues until you either land on a dropping tile (the stake is lost) or clear all three levels (the bet pays your difficulty's top multiplier and the egg hatches).
