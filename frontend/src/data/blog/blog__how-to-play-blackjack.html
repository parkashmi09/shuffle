Blackjack has one goal: beat the dealer's hand without going over 21. What Is the Goal of Blackjack?

The goal of blackjack is to finish with a hand value higher than the dealer's without exceeding 21. Go over 21 and you bust, losing your bet immediately — even if the dealer later busts too.

Here's the part many new players miss: you are not competing with anyone else at the table. Other players' hands have no effect on whether you win. Every hand is a private contest between you and the dealer, which is what makes blackjack simpler than it first looks — and different from a game like baccarat, where you bet on an outcome rather than playing a hand yourself. Blackjack Card Values

Number cards are worth their face value, jacks, queens and kings are each worth 10, while an ace counts as 1 or 11 — whichever helps your hand more.

Suits don't matter in blackjack. A hand's value is simply the sum of its cards, so a king and a 7 make 17, while an ace and a 5 make either 6 or 16.

That flexible ace creates two types of hands worth knowing by name. A soft hand contains an ace counted as 11 — like ace-6, a "soft 17" — meaning you can't bust with one more card, since the ace drops to 1 if needed. A hard hand has no ace, or an ace that must count as 1. The distinction matters most when you start making decisions, which is where strategy takes over from the rules.

How a Round of Blackjack Works

A round of blackjack follows the same sequence every time:

  1. Place your bet. Each seat has a betting area with posted minimum and maximum stakes.

  2. The deal. You receive two cards face up. The dealer takes two cards — one face up (the upcard), one face down (the hole card).

  3. Check for blackjack. If your first two cards are an ace plus a 10-value card, that's a blackjack, or "natural." Unless the dealer also has one, you win right away.

  4. Make your decisions. Starting from the dealer's left, each player acts: hit, stand, double down, split or surrender. Take as many cards as you like — until you stand or bust.

  5. The dealer plays. Once all players finish, the dealer reveals the hole card. The dealer must draw until reaching at least 17, then must stand. There are no dealer decisions.

  6. Payouts. Beat the dealer's total and win even money on your bet. A blackjack traditionally pays 3:2. Tie the dealer and it's a push — your bet is returned. Bust or finish lower, the bet is lost.

Player Actions Explained

Every decision in blackjack comes down to six possible actions. Not all of them are available on every hand, but you should know all six before you play.

Hit

Take one more card. You can keep hitting until you stand or your total passes 21. Hit when your hand is low enough that another card is more likely to help than hurt.

Stand

Take no more cards and lock in your total. The turn passes on, then the dealer plays.

Double Down

Double your bet in exchange for exactly one more card. It's a way to press your advantage on strong starting totals — typically 10 or 11 — when one good card can make a near-unbeatable hand.

Split

If your first two cards are a matching pair, you can split them into two separate hands, placing a second bet equal to your first. Each hand is then played independently. A pair of aces or eights is the classic split; a pair of tens is the classic hand to leave alone.

Surrender

Give up your hand immediately and reclaim half your bet. It's the right move only in a handful of very weak spots — and note that not every table offers it, so check the rules before you sit.

Insurance

When the dealer's upcard is an ace, you'll be offered insurance: a side bet, up to half your original stake, that pays 2:1 if the dealer has blackjack. Here's the honest version — insurance carries a house edge well above the base game, which is why most experienced players decline it every time. Treat it as a bet you can safely ignore.

Blackjack Payouts and Table Rules

Blackjack pays even money on a standard win — bet $10, win $10. A natural blackjack traditionally pays 3:2, so a $10 bet returns $15 in winnings.

Some tables pay 6:5 on blackjack instead. The difference sounds small but isn't: that same $10 bet wins only $12, which meaningfully raises the house edge over a session. If you see both options, the 3:2 table is always the better game.

Two more rules vary from table to table, so read the felt before you play:

  • Soft 17. Some tables have the dealer stand on all 17s; others have the dealer hit soft 17. A dealer who stands on soft 17 is slightly better for you.

  • Deck count. Blackjack runs on anywhere from one to eight decks. Fewer decks generally favor the player, though table rules matter more than deck count alone.

None of these rules require memorization to enjoy the game — but knowing them is how you pick the right table instead of the first one you see.

Do These Rules Apply to Every Blackjack Game?

Yes — everything in this guide carries over to every blackjack variant: the card values, the goal of 21, the dealer's fixed draw rules, plus the standard player actions. What changes from game to game sits on top of that foundation:

  • Speed and multihand tables change the pace, not the rules. Speed tables deal to whoever decides fastest; multihand games let you play several hands in the same round, each played out independently.

  • Infinite-style tables deal one shared starting hand that every player then plays their own way — your decisions remain entirely yours. These tables often add the Six Card Charlie rule: draw six cards without busting and you win automatically, even against a dealer blackjack.

  • Lightning-style tables keep standard rules and payouts but add a mandatory fee equal to your bet, which funds random multipliers (2x–25x) applied to your next winning hand. Factor that in before comparing stakes — a $10 bet costs $20 to place at these tables.

  • Side bets like pairs or 21+3 are optional extra wagers with their own payout tables. They typically carry a higher house edge than the main game, so treat them as entertainment on top, never as the core bet.

Common Beginner Mistakes

  • Taking insurance. Covered above — it looks protective, it isn't.

  • Playing 6:5 tables without noticing. Check the payout line printed on the table before betting.

  • Copying other players. Their cards don't affect your hand. Play your own total against the dealer's upcard.

  • Standing on everything out of caution. Refusing to hit a 12 against a dealer 10 loses more over time than the occasional bust costs.

  • Playing by feel. Every starting hand has a mathematically best response. Learning even a simplified blackjack strategy chart removes the guesswork — it's the single biggest upgrade a new player can make.

FAQ

What are the rules of blackjack?

You and the dealer each receive two cards, then you decide to hit, stand, double down, split or surrender, aiming for a total closer to 21 than the dealer's without going over. The dealer must draw to 16 and stand on 17. A winning hand pays even money; a two-card 21 (blackjack) traditionally pays 3:2.

How much is a king worth in blackjack?

A king is worth 10, as are queens and jacks. All face cards carry the same value, so a king-queen hand totals 20.

What is an ace worth in blackjack?

An ace is worth 1 or 11 — whichever benefits your hand. An ace with a 7 can be 8 or 18; if you then draw a 9, the ace automatically counts as 1, making 17 instead of busting.

Does the dealer win on a tie in blackjack?

No. A tie is called a push: nobody wins, nobody loses, your bet is returned. The only exception at some tables involves specific side rules, which is another reason to read the table rules before playing.

Is blackjack a game of skill or luck?

Both. The cards you're dealt are pure chance, but your decisions genuinely change your results — blackjack played with correct decisions has one of the lowest house edges in the casino, while careless play hands that edge right back. No skill level eliminates the house edge entirely, so treat every session as entertainment first.
